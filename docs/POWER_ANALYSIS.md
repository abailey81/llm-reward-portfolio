**The power problem (state it plainly).** The unit of inference is a reward function's OOS
risk-adjusted performance, not the cross-section of assets. "~2,000 test-span trading days" is NOT
2,000 independent observations — daily returns are autocorrelated and **regime-clustered**. The number
of *independent regimes* in 2005–2025 is **single digits** (GFC, the 2010s low-vol bull, 2018 vol
spikes, COVID, the 2022 drawdown). So regime-stratified inference has limited power, and a per-regime
null can arise from data scarcity alone. This is why PBO/CSCV is the *primary* guard and why the claim
is comparative, not absolute.

## Method (sketch)
1. Label regimes; count independent blocks (autocorrelation-aware).
2. From the pilot, estimate the noise in the headline metric across seeds within an arm.
3. Monte-Carlo: under H0 and under a range of true effect sizes, simulate the arm-level metric with the
   estimated noise and the regime count; compute the bootstrap-difference test's rejection rate.
4. Report the minimum detectable effect and whether the planned budget reaches 80% power; if not, say so
   and record that the contribution may rest on a rigorous null.
