## (a) Epistemic framing of the null: Popperian → Mayoian (adopted)

I have reframed how the dissertation credits its (likely) null result. The change is **editorial, not
scientific** — the equivalence-testing machinery (TOST against a pre-registered SESOI) already implements
the new framing; only the prose around it changes.

- **Was:** the null framed as a *corroborated Popperian prediction* about the gap between the
  information-theoretic envelope and its bounded realisation.
- **Now:** the null credited on **error-statistical severity** (Mayo) — the frozen, deviation-free
  cryptographic protocol admits no sample-based deviations and hence no unknown Type-I inflation — together
  with **garden-of-forking-paths avoidance** (the whole analysis plan is fixed in advance). It is reported
  as a **TOST equivalence against the pre-registered SESOI**, not as a bare *p* > 0.05.

**Cite basis** (all to be cross-checked against `paper/refs.bib` before they enter the PDF):
- **Rubin (2025, *Synthese*, arXiv:2408.12347)** — pre-registration does *not*, on its own, improve the
  severity of a Popperian test; a deviation-free protocol is what supports Mayoian severity.
- **Mayo (2018)** — error-statistical severity.
- **Gelman & Loken (2014)** — the garden of forking paths (multiple-comparisons invalidity without
  conscious p-hacking).
- **Lakens (2018)** and **Campbell & Gustafson (2018)** — equivalence testing / a bare non-significant
  result is not itself evidence for the null.

If you see any over-claim or mis-scoping in this framing, I would welcome a correction.
