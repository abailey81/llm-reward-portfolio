## 2. The diagnosis, tested rather than assumed

The brief's hypothesis is that the document is a local optimum of criterion-wise hill-climbing
against a word limit that excludes apparatus. I tested it three ways and it survives, with one
refinement.

**Test 1 — is the free material really where the pages are?** Yes, and more sharply than stated.
Of 317 pages, **184 are word-excluded apparatus**: 149 appendix pages plus 35 body pages that are
six standalone table files (`paper/tables/T_*.md`) appended to Chapters 2, 4 and 5. The body's
own prose, at 10,951 words on a 453.5pt column at 12pt/1.5, occupies roughly 34 pages. Everything
else in the body is exhibit.

**Test 2 — is the free material that requires taste conspicuously thin?** Partly. Tables number
49 against 12 figures, and the ratio is the predicted one. But the prediction fails on
mathematics: the document contains a 26-page, genuinely first-rate decision-theoretic appendix
(Blackwell dominance, Le Cam deficiency stated as an identity, Kusuoka spanning, joint
elicitability, and an honestly-labelled assumption (NI) that is stated rather than smuggled).
**The mathematics is not thin. It is misplaced.** It was moved out of the body on 2026-08-01 to
meet the word limit, which is exactly the gradient at work, but the material itself is the
strongest Criterion-1 and Criterion-3 asset the document owns.

**Test 3 — do exhibits cost counted words?** Yes. Every exhibit carries a prose lead-in, and 49
tables carry roughly 49 sentences whose only job is to announce a table. Cutting exhibits
therefore frees words as well as pages, which is why the redesign can afford to move theory
*into* the body.

**The refinement, and it is the fourth consequence the brief predicts.** The gradient acted on
the previous pass too. Appendix G, *Extended notes*, is 31 pages of twenty-eight numbered notes,
each answering one objection at zero word cost. Appendix H is 24 pages sourcing the cells of one
literature table. Together that is **55 pages, 17.4% of the document**, whose entire content is
the answer to questions nobody has yet asked on the page.

## 3. What the marking criteria actually say about this

Read first-hand from `MSc_Project_Marking_Criteria_2019-20.pdf`, 4 dimensions x 9 bands,
36 descriptor cells, and **no weighting, aggregation or capping rule anywhere in the document**
(searched for weight, weighted, average, aggregate, overall, combine, cap, minimum, equal: zero
hits). The operative posture is therefore that all four dimensions must independently reach the
target.

Two descriptors govern this redesign, and both are verbatim:

- Communication, 50–59%: *"Adequate write-up, lacking clarity or detail in places, or containing
  **irrelevant material**"*.
- Communication, 40–49%: *"Write up is not fully coherent, rushed, contains important omissions
  or **irrelevant material**"*.

**"Irrelevant material" is named in the two failing bands of the dimension the document is
weakest on.** Cutting is not a concession to a page target. It is the direct defence of
Criterion 4, whose top band is *"faultless presentation of data"*.

The dissertation guide (`MSc_Project_Guidelines_2025-2026.pdf`, all 13 pages, read first-hand)
adds four binding facts:

1. **Sixteen sections, in order**, of which only *Data (If applicable)* and *Appendices (if
   necessary)* are conditional (p. 8–10). Glossary, nomenclature, abbreviations, ethics
   statements, AI disclosures, contributions lists and limitations chapters are **not among
   them**; the glossary and abbreviations list are named once, in the word-count exclusions
   (p. 11), which permits them without requiring them.
2. **No page limit exists anywhere in the guide.** The 130 is Tamer's constraint, not UCL's.
3. **Core ≈ 60%** of the dissertation is methodology, results and discussion (p. 8).
4. Typography: Arial or Helvetica *recommended*, **no less than 10pt**; 1.5 spacing for main
   text, *"except for indented quotations, tables, and footnotes, which may be single-spaced"*
   (p. 10). That exception was already taken on 2026-08-11, so it is not available again.

## 5. The decision principles

Stated crisply enough that a third party could apply them and reach the same answers.

**P1 — Higher standard for word-excluded material than for prose.** An exhibit must name one
claim, be necessary to the argument, replace prose, and be in the right form. Failing any of the
four, it goes.

**P2 — A table every one of whose data cells reads "sealed" is not a table.** It is an intention.
Registered verdict slots are consolidated into one place, not spread over four exhibits.

**P3 — A note that answers an unasked objection belongs in the sentence it defends or nowhere.**
An appendix section with no pointer from the body has already failed. An appendix section whose
only pointer is a footnote saying "this is set out in Appendix G.n" has failed differently: the
body sentence should carry the fact or drop the claim.

**P4 — Move the evidence out of the prose, never the argument.** A marker who opens no appendix
must still follow the whole case.

**P5 — Where the same fact is stated twice, it is stated once, in the place a marker meets first.**

**P6 — Material that carries a band moves toward the reader, not away.** The theory and the
difficulty denominator both currently sit in appendices. Both come forward.

**P7 — Nothing that runs against the author's interest is cut.** The five owned faults, the
disclosed limitations, the superseded measurements, the assumption (NI), the (unverifiable)
abstention claim: these are where the document earns its credibility, and they are the worst
possible page savings.

## 10. What could go wrong, and the guard

| risk | guard |
|---|---|
| a cut removes the evidence behind a band invisibly | every deletion names what it served and where that service now happens; the mark-attribution map is re-checked on the final build |
| deleting Appendix G orphans 27 body footnotes | each footnote is resolved individually: the fact moves into the sentence, or the claim it defends is weakened to what the body can support |
| deleting Appendix B sections breaks a hash-bound pointer | `PREREGISTRATION.md`, `config/preregistration.yaml` and `src/feedback/schema.py` cite B section numbers. Those sections keep their numbers. Verified before any edit to B |
| the sealed verdicts have nowhere to land on 2026-08-27 | one consolidated verdict exhibit, with a row per registered endpoint, survives by design |
| a renumber leaves a dangling reference | `exhibit_pages.py --verify` and the presentation sweep run on every build, and the cross-reference check runs over the assembled deliverable |
| the register drifts while prose is edited | re-measured after every pass, over every compiled surface and not only the seven body chapters |
